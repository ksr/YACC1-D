### How it was routed

`ROUTE=1 build.sh` (about 40-60 minutes) rebuilds the board from `options/io-v2.0-option-b.kicad_pcb`:

1. **`route_v2.py prep`** copies option B and **locks** the v1.1 copper that option B kept (900 track segments and
   vias). KiCad exports locked items as Specctra "fix" wires, so Freerouting routes around them and never moves them.
   It also moves JP2 (below), tidies the reference texts and draws the adapter strip on F.Fab.
2. **Stage A:** Freerouting 1.9 (`-mt 1`, watchdog) routes only the 49 signal nets that have a broken v1.1
   connection (`reports/route-stage-a-nets.txt`: the nets that must thread between the kept copper). Freerouting
   reliably leaves about a dozen of these open; the grid router (step 4) finishes them there and then. Then all of it is
   locked.
3. **Stage B:** Freerouting routes everything else: the CF section, the rest of the v1.1 nets and the power nets.
4. **`route_v2.py finish --rip`**, a small grid maze router written for this board (0.127 mm grid, 0/45/90-degree
   tracks, v1.1's vias, A* with bend and via costs), routes whatever Freerouting left open. When a connection is
   walled in by router copper it may **rip up** router tracks of other nets and route those again (negotiated
   congestion); it never touches the kept v1.1 copper. Dangling router stubs are removed.
5. `import` proves after each Freerouting pass that every kept v1.1 item is still there, bit for bit;
   `route_check.py` proves it again on the finished board.

Freerouting is not deterministic, so each `ROUTE=1` run gives a different, equally valid routing. **The committed
`io-v2.0.kicad_pcb` is the master.** A plain `build.sh` does not re-route. It verifies the committed board (DRC,
kept-copper check, netlist proof) and regenerates the fab files. If you edit the board in pcbnew, do not run
`ROUTE=1` again. Re-run `build.sh` to check your edit.

The router's tracks are functional, not pretty: the grid-routed ones have more jogs and vias than a hand route.
Tidy them in pcbnew if you like, then re-run `build.sh`.

## The CF adapter: TAODAN CF-IDE40

Ken's adapter is a TAODAN "CF-IDE40 V2.0":

- 70 x 63 mm PCB;
- a **female** 40-pin 2.54 mm socket along one 70 mm edge, and the CF slot on the opposite edge;
- its own power / card-detect / active LEDs;
- a white 4-pin floppy power connector;
- master/slave and 5 V / 3.3 V jumpers;
- it takes power from IDE pin 20 or from its own connector, whichever is present.

**It plugs straight onto J2 (no ribbon) and stands perpendicular to the I/O card, out of the component side.** So
there are **no mounting or standoff holes**, and none are planned.

- J2 is a straight (vertical) shrouded 2x20 male header; the shroud keys the plug. Pin 20 is present: JP2 fitted
  puts +5 V on it, which is how the adapter is powered here.
- **Set the adapter's jumpers to master and 5 V.**
- **Space:** the I/O card needs about **75 mm of free space** on its component side: an end slot, or empty
  neighbour slots.
- J3 (+5 V / GND / GND / n.c., for other adapters) and the ACT LED (LED1 + R15) stay.

**Mechanical check.** The adapter's PCB edge sits about 9-10 mm above the board, because its socket body rests on
J2's shroud. Its 70 mm span is centred on J2 (x 41.9 to 111.9; J2's pins span x 52.7 to 101.0), so it overhangs each
end of J2 by about 10 mm. Ken's rule: nothing taller than about 8 mm within 12 mm beyond either end of J2, along J2's
axis (x 35.1 to 118.6, near y 10-21).

| Part | Where | Height | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| JP2 (1x2 header + shunt) | was x 38.7-44.9, under the overhang | ~8.5 mm | **too tall -> moved** to x 29.2-32.8 (14 mm beyond J2's end, clear) |
| RN3 (SIP9 10k network) | right end at x 43.5, y 12.7-15.9 | ~5 mm | OK (under the left overhang, 4-5 mm clearance) |
| IC15 (74LS245, DIP-20) | x 107.2-117.0 from y 12.4 | ~5 mm soldered, ~7.5-8.5 mm in a socket | OK soldered or in a low-profile (turned-pin) socket; with a tall socket, clearance to the adapter edge is ~1 mm - **check with the real parts** |
| S7 (toggle switch) | from x 117.7 | tall | clear: 5.8 mm beyond the adapter's end |
| J2 itself | | shroud ~9 mm | the adapter sits on it |

Nothing else is within the zone.

**Which way the adapter faces** follows from J2's orientation as placed:

- J2's **pin 1 is at its right end**, x = 101.0, next to IC15, marked by the silk triangle at the board edge.
- J2's **key notch faces the card's top edge** (board y = 10, the edge beside RN3/J2). The odd pins 1-39 are in the
  row nearer that edge.
- The adapter's key goes into that notch. On the adapter, the PCB sits on one side of the socket:
  - if the PCB is on the same side as the key, the adapter **stands over the top-edge side of J2**, between J2 and the
    card edge. Only RN3's end (5 mm) is under it. This is the better way;
  - if the PCB is on the other side, it stands over the **interior side**, above the strip between J2 and IC3/IC10
    (no part there taller than J2) and above IC15's pin-1 corner (see the table).

  Both work electrically and mechanically as placed. Ken: check your adapter. If you want it facing the other way,
  turn J2 by 180 degrees (pin 1 at the RN3 end). That needs a re-route (`ROUTE=1 build.sh` after changing J2's
  rotation in `placements.py` option B, or in `route_v2.py FINAL_MOVES`).

## What changed from v1.1

- **Circuit:** v1.1 is unchanged, pin for pin. The CF section is added: the CF card v1.0 circuit **minus** its 74LS138
  (U1), bus connector (X1), U1's capacitor, PWR LED (the card already has PWR0 + R2) and DASP LED (Ken). v1.1 **has** a
  power LED, so the CF card's PWR LED was not carried over.
- **Ports:** the CF decode now comes from the I/O card's own **IC5 (74LS138)**. **Y4 (pin 11, -IO-SEL4) = P4** is the
  register-select latch (write: bits 0-2 = ATA register DA0-2, bit 3 = CF reset). **Y5 (pin 10, -IO-SEL5) = P5** is the
  data port (read/write). They replace the CF card's -P8SEL / -P9SEL.
  - On v1.1, Y4 and Y5 connected only to the IO-ADDR and DATA-ADDR jumper headers (pins 8 and 6). They had no
    consumer on the card, so they were not literally unconnected. `check_netlist.py` prints and verifies this.
  - **The IO-ADDR-HL strap must be in P0-P7** (jumpers 3-5 and 4-6, the "L" end).
  - **The IO-ADDR / DATA-ADDR jumpers must never select P4 or P5.**
  - Both rules are in the silkscreen text and in notes on schematic sheets 5 and 7.
- **Schematic:** sheets 1-6 are v1.1's, copied, with two edits on sheet 5:
  - the four -IO-SEL4 / -IO-SEL5 labels became boxed global labels (only global labels cross sheets);
  - a strap note was added.

  Sheet 7 is the CF section. It is drawn by the CF card's own sheet writer (`gen_cf.py`, class `Sheet`, reused
  read-only) in the same style: stubs to labels, and global labels for the shared nets DATA0-7, -IO-RD, -IO-WR,
  -RESET, -IO-SEL4 and -IO-SEL5.
- **Board:** the same outline (177.83 x 114.02 mm) and X1 position as v1.1, because it starts from the v1.1 board file,
  so it fits the same backplane. The v1.1 footprints and their copper are kept. v1.1 copper that a move or a new part
  broke is removed (see [the options](#the-placement-options-the-review-record)). The board title silk reads
  "YACC1 IO V2.0".

## Rebuild

    hardware/cards/io/kicad/v2.0/build.sh                   # ~1 minute: verify the committed board + fab files
    ROUTE=1 hardware/cards/io/kicad/v2.0/build.sh           # re-route from option B (40-60 minutes)
    OPTIONS="a b c" hardware/cards/io/kicad/v2.0/build.sh   # regenerate the review options in options/

1. `gen_io_v2.py sch` copies the v1.1 sheets and libraries, writes sheet 7 and the project (with the Power net class).
2. ERC gate.
3. (`OPTIONS`) Per option: `gen_io_v2.py board | trim | check | review`, then DRC and the review images.
4. (`ROUTE`) `route_v2.py prep`, Freerouting stage A, grid router, Freerouting stage B, `route_v2.py finish --rip`
   ([How it was routed](#how-it-was-routed)).
5. DRC of `io-v2.0.kicad_pcb` against v1.1, with these gates: 0 unconnected; no new copper violation; no silk
   collision on a new or moved reference; every kept v1.1 item intact (`route_check.py`).
6. `check_netlist.py` proof (the schematic, the routed board and the three option boards).
7. Fab outputs (`tools/kicad/kicad_route.py finish`): `gerbers/` + drill + `io-v2.0-gerbers.zip`, the top render, the
   placement PDF; then the bottom render, the schematic PDF and the BOM.

The steps need:

- KiCad 10 (kicad-cli and its Python);
- Freerouting 1.9 at `~/freerouting/freerouting.jar` (only for `ROUTE=1`);
- Inkscape (only for `OPTIONS`).

The committed board was made by the `ROUTE=1` flow on 2026-09-23/24. While it ran, the grid router got its
net-protection rule. Two nets kept taking one corridor from each other, so the last step was run again on the same
board (`reports/route-finish.txt`).

To change the circuit, edit `io_v2_netlist.py`, then run `build.sh`. The netlist proof will then fail against the
committed board until the board is updated: edit it in pcbnew, or re-route with `ROUTE=1`.
