# Design review — video card V4.0 before ordering (2026-11-13)

Three independent passes over `hardware/cards/video/kicad/v4.0` (the netlist of 2026-11-12, simulated; the board
routed and optimised 2026-11-13, commit `feb171b`; not ordered, no order files yet): the circuit, the timing, the
board. Findings only: the design files (`v40_netlist.py`, `gen_v40.py`, the KiCad project, the simulation, the
design note) are unchanged by this review; every finding carries a proposed fix.

**Result: HIGH 0, MED 4, LOW 12, INFO 7. Not to be ordered as it is.** Nothing found says the card will not work.
Two of the four MED findings (V4-1, V4-2: the clocks' wiring) want parts added and so a new route; V4-3 wants the
VGA socket's part chosen and checked against its footprint; V4-4 is a rule in the design note, no board change.

**After the fixes (2026-11-13, the status table below):** V4-3 and V4-4 are closed and the LOW findings that asked
for a change have it. V4-1 and V4-2 have their series resistors and the column moved; the clocks' lengths on the new
route are mixed (D2's inputs half as far, the dot clock's farther), and routing the four clocks first is open.

**What was read.**
- *The circuit:* `v40_netlist.py` pin by pin, every IC's pinout and function table re-derived (the 74S85, 74LS138,
  74LS32, 74HCT273, 74LS244, 74HCT04 / 08, the 6845, 74F163A, 74F74, 74ACT04, the IDT7134, 74ACT574, AT28C64B,
  74F299, 74F21, 74ACT86 / 08 / 157 / 244 / 32 / 00 / 11 / 02, the AS6C1008, 74HCT244 / 74, the ATmega328P,
  74LS374, 74HCT374, 7416, the oscillator can, the DE-15 and the Mini-DIN); the logic against
  `docs/cards/video-v4.md` sections 5-9; `docs/system/BUS.md`, `MICROCODE.md`, `MACHINE.md`; the V2.0 netlist and
  its three reviews; the PS/2 firmware `embedded/video-ps2`; `BOM.md`.
- *The timing:* `tests/video/v40_netsim.py` and its engine `v30_netsim.py`, run as they are (48 runs, all passing,
  the margins the note gives) and in changed copies (other bus delays, finer phases); the critical paths redone by
  hand from the TI sheets of the SN74F299 (SDFS071B), SN74F74 (SDFS046A), CD74ACT157 (SCHS340D) and SN74ACT574
  (SCAS537F), whose figures in the simulation all match; `tests/video/v20_timing.py` and the microcode image
  `test.hex` for what the bus does; the 6845's tables of the note's section 3 against VGA's timing.
- *The board:* `video-v4.0.kicad_pcb` through KiCad 10's `pcbnew` (pads, track lengths and paths per net, vias,
  copper to the edge, the planes' fills), KiCad's DRC run again, renders of both sides with the parts fitted,
  `gen_v40.py`, the blank card V3.2 and the V2.0 board for the outline and the connector, JLCPCB's published
  4-layer capabilities, the board-layout rules of 2026-10-24 and after.

Each finding was then checked again before it was accepted: the lengths of V4-1, V4-2 and V4-11 measured on the
board file a second time, the limits of V4-4 and V4-6 and the hold of V4-5 re-run in the simulation, the title
block of V4-15 read from the board. Where a figure is one pass's measurement only, it says so.

Severity as in the V2.0 reviews: **HIGH** = will not work, damages parts, or must be fixed before ordering;
**MED** = likely problem or no margin by specification; **LOW** = improvement; **INFO** = a fact the next step
(the driver, the purchase, the bench) needs.

Data-sheet values. Fetched for this card: the TI sheets named above and the CD74ACT157 / SN74ACT244 (for the
simulation), Renesas' 7134, Atmel's AT28C64B, a scan of Motorola's MC6845. Not fetched: the AS6C1008's (its figures
are the tree's AS6C62256 sheet's family values, as the V3 check used them), the 6845's bus-side figures (as V2.0's
reviews quote them), Hitachi's HD6845S. Those are marked To verify where a finding rests on them.

## Findings

### V4-1 MED — The dot clock: seven 74F clock inputs on 177 mm of branched, unterminated track

**Where:** net CK, from OSC1 pin 8 at (170.3, 111.4).

**Evidence.** 177 mm of track on one layer, no vias, seven loads. Along the copper from the oscillator: IC20.11
25 mm, IC19.12 44, IC11.2 58, IC33.12 80, IC12.3 88, IC35.3 156, IC35.11 166 mm. About 21 pF of track (0.25 mm over
the plane) and seven 74F clock inputs: 55 to 60 pF by estimate. Oscillators of this kind are commonly rated for
15 pF, some for 50 pF or 10 TTL loads; the BOM says only "TTL / HCMOS output". 166 mm is about 1.1 ns one way,
comparable to the edge: a line with branches and nothing to terminate it. An edge that is not monotonic at IC11's
clock counts a dot twice, which breaks the count and the 6845's clock; at a shifter it loses a dot. The simulation
takes CK as an ideal edge and every wire as having no length.

**Fix.** Before ordering: a series resistor footprint (22 to 33 ohm) at OSC1 pin 8 - one part and one net more,
and so a new route; in the BOM an oscillator rated for 50 pF or 10 TTL loads. On a built board: scope CK at IC35
pins 3 and 11 and at IC11 pin 2.

### V4-2 MED — D2, D3 and STG: clocks 240 to 270 mm long from 74F and 74ACT outputs, unterminated

**Where:** D2 (IC11 pin 12), D3 (IC11 pin 11), STG (IC13 pin 2).

**Evidence.** Measured on the board:

| Net | Track | Vias | Along the copper from the source |
|---|---|---|---|
| D2 | 267 mm | 4 | IC38.11 21, IC20.3 40, IC34.3 56, IC36.1 86, IC21.9 98, IC13.3 102, IC32.11 126, IC17.11 197 mm |
| D3 | 241 mm | 2 | IC12.2 39, IC12.11 49, IC13.1 65, IC14.6 106, IC24.2 128 mm |
| STG | 239 mm | 3 | IC22.4 39, IC16.11 160, IC15.11 200 mm |

D2 clocks the code register IC17, the byte latch IC32, the cursor flag IC20/A and the write flip-flop IC34/A; D3
the DE flip-flop IC12/B; STG both stage registers. With edges of 1.5 to 3 ns these are lines with stubs, and a
non-monotonic edge at a 74F74's or a 74ACT574's clock can clock it twice. (For D2 and STG the data does not move
within a few nanoseconds of the edge, so a second clocking takes the same value: the risk is smaller than CK's.)
The cause is the placement: the counter's column stands at x = 154, the stage and the code register at x = 93 -
the columns were reordered for the text RAM after the first routing, and the clock's column was not moved with
them. The wires' delays, up to 1.3 ns, are not in the simulation; they are of the size of the hold margin of V4-5.

**Fix.** Series resistor footprints at IC11 pin 12, IC11 pin 11 and IC13 pin 2; and / or the clock's column (IC21,
IC13, IC12, IC11, the oscillator under it) moved between the multiplexers and the RAM's latches, which about
halves these paths. The simulation to take a delay for each wire (about 6.5 ps a millimetre). A new route either
way. On a built board: scope D2 at IC17 pin 11 and STG at IC15 pin 11.

### V4-3 MED — J1, the VGA socket: no part named, the footprint not checked against one

**Where:** J1; `BOM.md`'s row "VGA".

**Evidence.** The footprint is KiCad's `DSUB-15-HD_Socket_Horizontal_P2.29x1.90mm_EdgePinOffset3.03mm_Housed_
MountingHolesOffset4.94mm`: the rows 1.90 mm apart, the front row 3.03 mm from the board's edge, two 3.2 mm lock
holes 25.0 mm apart and 4.94 mm in. Right-angle HD-15 sockets come in at least two patterns that do not fit each
other (KiCad's other one: rows 2.54 mm apart, the pins 8.35 mm from the edge), and `gen_v40.py` itself says "TO
VERIFY ... (1.90 mm here; 1.98 on some makers' drawings)". The BOM asks for "DE-15 (HD-15) socket, right-angle PCB
mount, 3.03 mm from the pins to the edge": no maker, no number. No data sheet was found that confirms 1.90 mm and
3.03 mm together.

What is right: the socket faces outwards, its flange on the edge (267.75 against 267.747), the pins 1 to 5 in the
row farthest from the edge, not mirrored; red, green and blue on 1, 2, 3, HSYNC on 13, VSYNC on 14, the grounds 5 to
8 and 10 and the shell, solid to the plane.

**Fix.** The part chosen (maker, number, the drawing's revision), its rows, pin-to-face distance and lock holes
checked against the footprint, and named in the netlist's `BUY`. Before ordering.

### V4-4 MED — The rules allow what fails: a stack or a BRVR vector table in the window, in slot mode above about 2.8 MHz

**Where:** `docs/cards/video-v4.md` section 7, rules 2 and 3 (section 13 says the same); `BUS_SLOW["addr"]` in
`tests/video/v40_netsim.py`.

**Evidence.** Rule 2 keeps a stack and a BRVR vector table out of the window at 6 MHz only; rule 3 allows slot mode
to 3.5 MHz. The simulation's bus puts the address on the bus 114 ns after the step's edge: a register selected in
that step. `tests/video/v20_timing.py` lists the one-step read windows whose address register was counted in the
step before - RET, IRET and POPR through SP, BRVR's second vector byte - and their address comes later: 218 ns
(85 C) for a count within the window, 256 ns for a count into it. The window's run with those delays, made again
for this review:

| The address after the step's edge | 3.5 MHz | 3.0 MHz | 2.8 MHz | Shortest step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 114 ns, a register selected (what is simulated) | passes, 42 ns | | | 531 ns, 3.76 MHz |
| 218 ns, counted within the window | fails (the read latch's hold) | passes, 36 ns | passes, 84 ns | 636 ns, 3.15 MHz |
| 256 ns, counted into the window | wrong bytes | wrong bytes | passes, 44 ns | 674 ns, 2.97 MHz |

**Fix.** Rule 2: no stack and no BRVR vector table in the window above about 2.8 MHz, in either mode (rule 1, no
code in the window, stands at every clock). The counted cases in the simulation's window runs and limit search. No
change to the board.

### V4-5 LOW — The smallest margin on the card is a hold time of 2.2 ns, and it is in no table

**Where:** the shifters IC19 and IC33 (74F299), which load as dots 4 and 12 begin from the character generator
(addressed by IC17) and from IC32 - which take their next byte on D2's rise, made by the same edge of the clock.

**Evidence.** The hold: the 74F163A's least clock-to-output, 2.7 ns, and the 74ACT574's, 1.5 ns (the character
generator adds its output hold, 0), less the 74F299's data hold, 2.0 ns: 2.2 ns. Measured in the simulation for
this review: 2.2 ns with the parts fast, 2.7 with their rising outputs fast, 9.0 with the falling ones, 15 to 18
with the parts slow. The simulation fails a run on a hold violation, but its table lists set-up margins and one
hold only, so the note and the README give 5.6 ns as the smallest. The clock reaches IC33 22 mm after the counter
(0.15 ns the wrong way) and IC19 before it; typical parts leave about 9 ns.

**Fix.** The hold margins in the simulation's table, and in the note's. For a margin that does not rest on least
delays: the load a dot earlier (in dots 2 and 10: a whole dot of hold), which wants D0 inverted by a fast gate -
one of IC13's inverters freed by moving a slow signal to IC8's spare one - and leaves the load's set-up about
6 ns where it has 14.7. To weigh when the board is routed again.

### V4-6 LOW — Slot mode's read margin and limit are a little overstated

**Evidence.** The bus's operations start at 32 phases of a count, 19.9 ns apart (48 in the limit search): the worst
phase falls between them. With 160 phases a byte read at 3.5 MHz is on the bus 42 ns before it is taken, not 50;
with 128 the shortest step is 531 ns, 3.76 MHz, not 522 ns and 3.8. By hand: the address, the multiplexer (8.6)
and the RAM (55) just missing the read latch's clock, the next 318 ns later, the latch 12 ns: 396 ns after the
address; 114 + 396 = 510 against the take at 546.

**Fix.** The note's and the README's figures; 128 phases or more for the margins. 3.5 MHz stands for a selected
register (and see V4-4).

### V4-7 LOW — The cursor's address after scrolling

**Evidence.** The 6845 compares all 14 bits of its address counter with R14 / R15, and the text RAM takes only
MA0-9. The cursor's registers must hold the counter's value at the cursor's count: R12 / R13 + row x 40 +
column / 2, not wrapped at 1024. The note's section 3 and section 6 say (row x 80 + column) / 2, which is right
only while the start address is 0.

**Fix.** One line in the note; the driver.

### V4-8 LOW — The 6845's R/-W moves with E (V2.0's V2-15 and R3-2, still open)

**Evidence.** R/-W is -IO-WR itself (IC10 pin 22) and E is -IO-RD XOR -IO-WR through IC22/A: at an `OUT PA` or
`OUT PB` R/-W falls one gate's delay (1 to 10 ns) before E rises, against an address set-up of 70 to 160 ns by
grade, and rises again 1 to 10 ns before E falls (To verify on the sheet of the 6845 fitted). The scheme is V2.0's
and was proven at the bench on the V1.2 card. The simulation's `ports()` checks levels, not this timing. The rest
of the 6845's bus side holds: at 6 MHz E is 333 ns (220 wanted by the B grade), the data two steps before and one
after; a read's byte on the bus at 188 ns against the take at 308.

**Fix.** None on the board. Scope E against R/-W at the first `OUT PB`.

### V4-9 LOW — The three guns have no back-termination

**Evidence.** 470 ohm from IC39's pins 4, 10, 13 to J1's pins 1 to 3: 0.69 V into the monitor's 75 ohm, which is
right. The source is 470 ohm, so a reflection from a long or poor cable is not absorbed at the card.

**Fix.** None needed: the usual circuit for a one-bit picture. (Two resistors a gun would terminate it.)

### V4-10 LOW — Long runs beside the clocks

**Evidence** (the board pass's measurement: the same layer, 0.6 mm or less between centres; not measured again). CK
beside STG 30 mm; D2 beside -IO-WR 46 mm; D3 beside STG 30, -IO-WR 25 and SHS 23 mm; STG beside SCUR 34 mm; CRTCCK
beside GFXON 33 mm; P56 beside RDCKD 28 mm; GWE# beside GA9 32 and GA13 28 mm. Well under the length at which the
coupling stops growing for 2 ns edges: 0.1 to 0.2 V by estimate.

**Fix.** None. A look at CK and STG when the clocks are scoped; the runs change with the next route.

### V4-11 LOW — The card puts more track on the bus than V2.0

**Evidence.** DATA0-7: 364 to 486 mm each on this card (V2.0: 280 to 333 mm), about 45 to 60 pF of track a line
and 12 pins; -RESET 362 mm, -IO-WR 305 mm; -INT 291 mm, to JP1 at the front edge and back (the rule that puts the
jumper there).

**Fix.** None. A note for the bus's loading at higher CPU clocks.

### V4-12 LOW — The oscillator's pin 1 is marked only by its outline's square corner

**Evidence.** OSC1 stands in a symmetrical 4-pin socket; turned round, pin 14 (VCC) is on pin 7's place (GND)
and the oscillator is destroyed. The silk has the outline's one square corner, at the bottom left (152.2, 121.6),
and nothing else.

**Fix.** A "1" or a dot outside the outline at pin 1.

### V4-13 LOW — The text-only legend leaves out the capacitors

**Evidence.** The silk says "TEXT-ONLY BUILD: NO IC24-IC34, RN2"; the BOM also leaves C25-C35 off in that build.

**Fix.** The capacitors in the legend.

### V4-14 LOW — A few labels reach under a socket's frame by a tenth of a millimetre

**Evidence** (the board pass's measurement, a 300-mil socket's frame taken as 1.3 mm beyond the pads' centres; not
measured again). The labels of C41, C24, C34, C4, C42 / C44 and C43 reach 0.03 to 0.13 mm under the frames of IC7,
IC6, IC13, IC18, IC41 and IC46; the text-only legend ends at x = 198.47 under IC9's frame (from 198.34). All stay
readable. About 50 labels cross vias.

**Fix.** Optional: 0.3 mm each.

### V4-15 LOW — The board's title block still says "not routed"

**Evidence.** The board's comment reads "The first placement, 250 mm, not routed: gen_v40.py from v40_netlist.py"
(read from `video-v4.0.kicad_pcb`), and the placement drawing prints it. The placement drawn is the board's.

**Fix.** The comment in `gen_v40.py`'s `board()`.

### V4-16 LOW — The bill of materials

**Evidence.** No shunts are listed: SV1 wants one (5-6, the $D000 block), JP1 one for -INT. The oscillator's 4-pin
socket has no part named. J2 / J3 ask for an MD-60SM "date code after 2022-03-03", which a buyer can rarely choose.

**Fix.** Two 2.54 mm shunts; a socket named; for the jacks, "check the part against the footprint before
soldering".

### V4-17 INFO — The oscillator's pin 1 is left open

On most full-size cans pin 1 is not connected; on some it is an enable with a pull-up inside, and open means
running. Buy one whose sheet says either.

### V4-18 INFO — Copper under the metal-bodied parts

Under OSC1: one via (SCUR, at 164.42, 114.16) and tracks on both sides (CK, VIDNQ, SVS, BOARDSEL, SCUR; STATOE#,
TXTONLY, WRQ, CRTCE). Under Y1: XT1 and XT2 on the front, INTOC on the back. Under J1 and the two jacks: their own
signals. Fine under solder mask with the vias tented or plugged: order it so, as V2.0.

### V4-19 INFO — The 250 mm card's mechanics are not settled in the tree

The card is held by X1 and its two screws alone, and the connectors take the cables' forces 230 mm from them (V2.0
at 218 mm is held the same way). The VGA socket's shell stands 6.4 mm past the edge; the jacks' faces are on it.
The cage is radial (`BUS.md` section 7: eight slots, 144.78 mm from the centre), so neighbours do not meet; no
enclosure or card guide is dimensioned anywhere. No tool in the tree assumes 218 mm. To see at the machine: the
room in the slot, and what carries the free end.

### V4-20 INFO — Two revisions of the 6845's sheet

Older Motorola sheets give the clock 160 ns low and 200 ns high, 2.5 MHz, and 300 ns for DE, CURSOR and the syncs;
the note's 150 / 150 ns, 3.0 MHz and 250 ns are the later revision's. Either is covered: the clock's halves are at
least 311 ns, the stage has 590 ns of set-up. The note's "a whole dot of hold" at the stage is 22 ns at worst (STG
up to 20 ns after the edge, the 6845's clock at least 43 ns): positive with a 6845 that answers in no time.

### V4-21 INFO — The vertical sync

As made it runs from the staged VSYNC's rise, at a line's first count, to the HSYNC of the line after: 92 counts,
1.84 lines, 58.5 us; its leading edge is not on an HSYNC. The note says "about two lines". IC40 never sees SVS and
SHS move in the same count. To see on a monitor.

### V4-22 INFO — Availability

TI lists the SN74F21N as active (one distributor has stopped stocking it); the 74F299 and 74F163A in DIP were not
checked. TI marked the SN74ACT244N "last time buy" in 2026-11; a CD74ACT244E may stand in (To verify against the
timing's figures).

### V4-23 INFO — What the simulation leaves out of its set-up checks, and why each is safe

- **IC34/A's D**, the write strobe taken into WRQ: about 25 ns to resolve before P56 rises, ample for a 74F74.
  WRQ's own path to the RAM's -OE can only move it in a slot that reads nothing.
- **IC30, the read latch:** a slot in which the CPU's address moved is not the one whose byte is taken; the run
  times the one that is - for a selected address only (V4-4).
- **IC32 in direct mode, or in a count whose DE is low:** the shifter is held clear by GFX-ON = 0, and RA moves
  only in count 49, whose DE is low with R0 = 49 and R1 = 40.
- **Everything while a control register is written** (about four steps and two counts): right only with rules 4
  and 5 of the note - the instruction that writes control 1 leaves the window alone, and the mode is switched in
  the vertical blanking.
- **Two outputs on a net** are judged in the straight corners only. The worst case across lots of the RAM's -OE
  against the write buffer, worked by hand: 2.6 ns at most, under the 3 ns allowed.

## Checked and found right

**The circuit.** Every pin, polarity, enable and bit order of all 46 ICs against its data sheet; the 74F299s' mode
pins and bit order (bit 7 first); the multiplexers' sense; the IDT7134's two ports; the character generator's
address; the RAM's enables; the ATmega's pins against the firmware's. The decode ($D000 / $D800 by ADDR11, the six
ports), the control and status bits against the note's section 9 (the ID reads 0111). The clock, the stage, SDE2,
the load in dots 3 and 11; P56 in dots 5-6 and 13-14, RDCK in dots 8 and 0; -WE's two terms, the RAM's -OE, the
two read paths exclusive by GFX-ON; the graphics addresses; the cursor's half; the mix and the guns; both syncs
negative after -RESET. No two drivers can be on the data bus, the RAM's data lines or the ATmega's bus together,
in any state the control registers can power up in. Every TTL-level output drives only TTL-threshold inputs;
GFX-ON's load is about 2.8 mA from its 74HCT273, PU carries about 28 74F inputs at about 4.4 V; no input floats.
The text-only build: only GDOT, WRQ and TXTONLY cross from the left-off parts, each held by its resistor. The PS/2
section is V2.0's net for net. The BOM's quantities and socket counts.
